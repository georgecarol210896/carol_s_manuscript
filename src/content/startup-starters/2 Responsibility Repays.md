This becomes your second hiring quality after vision:

“Can this person be trusted with what is placed in their hands?”

Opening Narrative Draft

Grace and peace to you, till we meet again beneath His everlasting wings.

There is a quiet currency heaven values that resumes rarely measure well — responsibility.

Not brilliance.
Not charisma.
Not loud ambition.

Responsibility.

The willingness to carry what was assigned without being watched.
To guard another man’s vision as though it were sacred.
To finish what was started when excitement has already faded.

In startups, everyone wants authority. Few want stewardship.

Yet Scripture repeatedly reveals that God entrusts greater things only to those faithful in smaller ones.

Before David wore a crown, he protected sheep.
Before Joseph governed Egypt, he managed a prison faithfully.
Before the disciples carried the Gospel to nations, they were first trusted to gather fragments after feeding multitudes.

The Kingdom pattern is unmistakable:
Responsibility precedes elevation.

Key Bible Verses on Stewardship & Responsibility
Faithfulness in Little Things

“Whoever can be trusted with very little can also be trusted with much.”
— The Gospel of Luke 16:10

Stewardship Before Reward

“Well done, good and faithful servant… You have been faithful over a few things, I will make you ruler over many things.”
— The Gospel of Matthew 25:21

Required of Stewards

“It is required in stewards that one be found faithful.”
— First Epistle to the Corinthians 4:2

Work as Unto God

“Whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men.”
— Epistle to the Colossians 3:23

Diligence Leads to Honor

“Do you see a man skillful in his work? He will stand before kings.”
— Book of Proverbs 22:29

The Parable of the Talents

Responsibility is not merely keeping something safe — it is multiplying what was entrusted.

— The Gospel of Matthew 25:14–30

Startup Reflection

A responsible employee:

Protects deadlines without excuses.
Owns failures before shifting blame.
Builds even when recognition is absent.
Treats company resources with reverence.
Understands that trust is capital.

In Kingdom thinking, stewardship is worship.

The employee who quietly carries responsibility may not impress the world immediately, but heaven records faithfulness differently.

Because God does not merely ask:

“How successful were you?”

He asks:

“What did you do with what I gave you?”
